The Chester County, PA HVAC market

Heating systems across Chester County are all over the map. Older homes in the northern townships still run oil boilers, rural properties use propane, newer neighborhoods in Uwchlan and West Whiteland are mostly gas forced-air, and more homeowners are switching to heat pumps. The fix, and the parts on the truck, depend on knowing which one the caller has.

Towns and area codes we cover

Heating and cooling calls here come in from the townships around them, Spring City, Downingtown, Paoli, Kennett Square, Malvern, West Chester, Avondale, Oxford, Coatesville, West Grove, Berwyn, Phoenixville and Exton. Area codes: 610 and its overlay 484.

When the phones get busiest

The first real cold snap in late October or November brings a wave of no-heat calls from systems that sat all summer, along with tune-up requests that should have come in September. July and August heat waves flip it to no-cooling calls, often several on the same afternoon. Spring and fall are when replacement quotes and maintenance-plan bookings pile up.

Licensing and permits, in brief

Pennsylvania doesn't license HVAC contractors statewide, so requirements are set by each municipality, and permits and inspections follow the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code where the township enforces it. Technicians who handle refrigerant need EPA Section 608 certification, and most residential contractors register under Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act. General background only; confirm current rules locally.
