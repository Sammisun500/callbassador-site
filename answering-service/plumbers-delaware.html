The Delaware plumbing market

Delaware plumbing calls come from Wilmington rowhomes with old supply lines, student rentals around the University of Delaware in Newark, fast-growing developments in Middletown and Smyrna, and beach homes in Sussex County that sit empty in the off-season. Plenty of shops here also cross the line into southern Chester County and Cecil County, Maryland.

Towns and area codes we cover

Plumbing shops we set up here typically run calls to across New Castle, Rehoboth Beach, Hockessin, Bear, Pike Creek, Newark, Seaford, New Castle, Middletown, Georgetown, Milford, Lewes, Smyrna, Wilmington, Dover, Kent and Sussex counties. Area codes: 302, the single area code that covers the whole state.

When the phones get busiest

Winter freezes bring burst pipes, especially in vacant beach homes and rentals that weren't winterized. Summer brings heavy use at the beaches and in rentals. Late August, when students move into Newark, brings a run of landlord and tenant calls for leaks, clogs and water heaters.

Licensing and permits, in brief

Delaware licenses plumbers statewide through the Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners, part of the Division of Professional Regulation. Businesses also need a Delaware business license from the Division of Revenue, and permits come from the county or city where the work is done. General background only; confirm current rules locally.

What Sami asks on a Delaware call

Sami asks who owns the property and who is on site, whether the home is occupied or a seasonal rental, and where the water is coming from, so you can call the right person back.
