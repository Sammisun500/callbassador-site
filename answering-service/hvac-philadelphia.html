The Philadelphia HVAC market

Philadelphia heating is mostly hot-water and steam radiators in older rowhomes and twins, gas forced-air in newer construction, and mini-splits and window units in apartments and converted buildings. Each one breaks differently, and each one needs a different tech.

Towns and area codes we cover

Heating and cooling calls here come in from Northern Liberties, South Philly, Manayunk, the Northeast, Chestnut Hill, Mt. Airy, Port Richmond, Kensington, West Philly, Center City, Fishtown, Germantown, University City, from Mayfair to Somerton and Roxborough. Area codes: 215 and its overlays 267 and 445.

When the phones get busiest

The first cold week of the season brings boiler no-heat calls and radiator questions all at once. Summer heat waves, when the city's dense blocks hold heat overnight, bring urgent no-cooling calls, often for older relatives. Landlords call before heating season starts to get units checked.

Licensing and permits, in brief

HVAC work in Philadelphia is permitted and inspected through the Department of Licenses and Inspections, and contractors need city licensing for the work they do. Anyone handling refrigerant needs EPA Section 608 certification. Businesses working in the city also need a Commercial Activity License. General background only; confirm current rules locally.

What Sami asks on a Philadelphia call

Sami asks whether it is a boiler, radiators, forced-air or a mini-split, which floor and unit the problem is in, and whether anyone vulnerable is in the home, and flags extreme-heat and no-heat calls based on your rules.
