Live answering services do not share one price. They sell minutes, or calls, or a monthly base plus a rate past the bundle. Nights, holidays, setup, and overage are where similar-looking plans diverge. The prices below are from each company's own pricing page as of Nov 2026, checked on November 9, 2026. If a figure is no longer on the page, trust the page.

The models you will see

Per minute. You pay for receptionist time. A three-minute call and a nine-minute call do not cost the same. Ask whether hold time, transfer time, and the notes after the call are billed. A plan with a low included-minute count and a high overage rate gets expensive in a storm week.

Per call. Each answered call counts as one, short or long. That is easier to predict if your calls vary in length, and it can cost more if most calls are quick "are you open Saturday" questions. Ask whether spam and hangups count.

Monthly plan. Almost everyone wraps the rate in a monthly fee that includes a bundle. Pay-as-you-go is still often a monthly base, plus the per-minute or per-call rate with nothing included.

Overage. Minutes or calls past the bundle. This is the number to read twice. The headline plan price is not the bill if you pass the bundle every month.

After-hours, weekend, and holiday surcharges. Some services include 24/7 in the rate. Some charge more at night or on holidays, or they only answer on a daytime schedule unless you buy the extra coverage. A contractor who needs nights is buying a different product than a shop that only wants lunch-hour overflow.

Setup fees. A one-time fee to write the script and open the account. Some pages say there is no setup fee. Some list one on the smaller plans.

Compared with hiring a receptionist

The Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook reports that the median wage for receptionists and information clerks was $18.27 per hour in May 2025, which is $38,010 per year. The lowest 10 percent earned less than $13.83 per hour. The highest 10 percent earned more than $24.01 per hour. The handbook says those figures are from the Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program. Source: BLS, Receptionists, OEWS, May 2025. The page was last modified October 29, 2026, and we read it on November 9, 2026.

Median annual pay of $38,010 is wages only. Payroll taxes, insurance, and paid leave are not in that number. One employee also covers one shift. Evenings and weekends are additional hours or they are uncovered. An office manager who dispatches and invoices is a broader job than the receptionist occupation in that table. We are not attaching a second BLS wage for that broader job in this article.

A live answering bill and an AI receptionist bill can both sit well under a full-time wage and still be the wrong buy if you need a person at the counter. They can also be the right buy for the hours nobody is at a desk. A longer look at the three options, without a forced winner, is in answering service vs. AI receptionist vs. an office manager.

What to check before you pick a number

  • Minutes or calls, and what happens past the bundle.
  • Whether nights, weekends, and holidays are included or billed extra.
  • Setup fees, and whether the script can be changed later without a fee.
  • Whether hold time and after-call notes count.
  • Whether the caller is told the call may be recorded, and whether an AI voice identifies itself.
  • Whether you keep your business number. Forwarding is covered in this guide.

Price a busy month, not a quiet one, and listen to a test call before customers do.
