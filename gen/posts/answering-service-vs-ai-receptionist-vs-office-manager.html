A small plumbing, HVAC, electrical, or roofing company usually has three ways to cover the phone when everyone is on a job. Pay a live answering service. Use an AI receptionist. Hire someone in-house. Each one can take a message. They do not cost the same, they do not fail in the same way, and none of them is a technician in a truck. This is a comparison of the jobs, not a ranking with a winner for every shop.

What you are actually buying

The phone task is narrow: answer, follow instructions you approved, collect the name, number, address, and problem, flag emergencies you defined, and send a summary. Quoting is only for prices you already approved. Nobody answering the phone, live or AI, should invent an arrival time.

A live answering service uses people who do not work for your company. An AI receptionist follows hours, service area, emergency rules, and approved answers, including at 2 a.m., and it will repeat a bad instruction, so you listen to a test call first. An in-house receptionist knows the trucks and the regulars. They also go home. The phone still rings.

Live answering, from published pricing pages

Live services usually sell a monthly bundle of minutes or of calls, then an overage rate. Some add a setup fee. Some include nights and holidays in the same rate, and some do not. None of these numbers include sales tax or add-ons you opt into. Check the vendor site before you rely on a figure.

An AI receptionist

An AI receptionist is software plus a phone number you forward to. The monthly price is usually a flat plan with included minutes, not a wage. Callbassador's published plans, for home-service businesses, are Starter at $79 per month for 150 minutes, Pro at $149 per month for 400 minutes with appointment booking, and Crew at $249 per month for 800 minutes with booking for more than one technician. Every plan includes a 14-day trial, $0.00 due today, with a card on Stripe. If a promotional rate is offered, it is listed on the pricing page only. Sami is an AI receptionist. Calls start with a recording notice. We configure the line for you, and it does not answer live customer calls until you approve.

The tradeoff is judgment. A person at the desk can leave the script. An AI receptionist follows the emergency rule you wrote. Test it before customers do. It should not invent a price or an arrival time.

Hiring in-house

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook page for receptionists says the median wage for receptionists and information clerks was $18.27 per hour, or $38,010 per year, in May 2025. The lowest 10 percent earned less than $13.83 per hour and the highest 10 percent earned more than $24.01 per hour. The handbook states that these wage figures come from the Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program. Source: BLS, Receptionists, data from OEWS, May 2025. The handbook page was last modified October 29, 2026. We checked it on November 9, 2026.

That median is wages. It does not include payroll taxes, workers' compensation, health coverage, paid time off, a desk, or a phone. It is also a national figure for the receptionist occupation, not a quote for your county and not a quote for an office manager who dispatches crews and sends invoices. That broader job can pay more. This article does not cite a second occupation's wage, because we are not attaching a number we did not verify on a BLS table.

One person also covers one shift. Nights, weekends, and the day they are out are uncovered unless you hire more hours or send the overflow somewhere else. A live answering service and an AI receptionist are both ways to cover the hours a single hire does not work. They are not a substitute for someone who knows which truck has the compressor.

How to choose without a slogan

If you want a person who can handle odd questions and you can write a tight script, price a live service at the minutes or calls you actually receive, including overage, and ask whether nights are included. If you want the same answers every time, including at night, and you are willing to approve the script before it goes live, an AI receptionist is the closer fit. If the phone is also the dispatch desk and the front counter, an in-house hire does work a remote service will not see. Many shops use more than one: a person during the day, and forwarding after hours.

Keep your existing number, tell callers when a call is recorded, and do not promise when someone will call back. See missed after-hours calls and answering service cost.
